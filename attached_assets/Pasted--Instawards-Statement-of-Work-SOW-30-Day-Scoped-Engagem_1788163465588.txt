2. Instawards Overview & Intent
2.1 Instawards Purpose (for Builder Context)
Instawards are designed to support short, clearly scoped, execution-focused work that helps a project make tangible progress toward building on Stellar. Instawards are meant to fund specific, achievable outcomes that can be completed and demonstrated within 30 days or less.

3. Problem Statement & Objective
Problem Being Addressed
What specific problem, gap, or blocker is this Instaward intended to solve?
ByahéBITES Nearby — Hanapin ang kainan sa budget mo — is now being deployed with pilot LGUs as a real-time MSME discovery engine used by LGUs, creators, and TPB/DOT for culinary tourism circuits. However, the platform lacks a verifiable on-chain identity layer for: LGU-issued MSME verification, creator contribution proofs, trust signals for TPB/DOT dashboards, wallet-based compensation flows, and cross-regional consistency in MSME validation. Every trust signal in the platform today — MSME readiness, creator output, editorial validation — lives off-chain in a private database, invisible and unverifiable to anyone outside it. This gap prevents TPB/DOT from using ByahéBITES as national tourism infrastructure, because verification and contribution signals are still off-chain and cannot be independently audited.
Objective of This Instaward
In one or two sentences, what will be true at the end of 30 days if this Instaward is successful?
At the end of 30 days, ByahéBITES will have a live Soroban credential layer on Stellar testnet: an MSME Credential Contract, a Creator Contribution Contract, wallet onboarding flows, Soroban RPC contract-state lookup, and a working demo frontend/backend — with at least 10 real MSMEs and 12 real creators credentialed (≥ 22 net-new Stellar wallets) where chapter-supported participant onboarding succeeds, every record readable via a documented Soroban RPC query and every transaction hash resolvable by non-technical reviewers on Stellar Expert (Testnet). All work is testnet-only.

3.1 Existing Work & Current Gaps
ByahéBITES Nearby — Hanapin ang kainan sa budget mo — is not starting from zero. The platform is now being deployed with pilot LGUs as a real-time MSME discovery engine: the MSME registry and circuit-based discovery are live, the editorial pipeline (LIT Magazine at https://LIT.komiksguild.com, Kom8ks at https://kom8ks.com) is actively producing content about real MSMEs, and the React/Supabase stack already serves LGU and creator users. This Instaward adds an on-chain layer to a running platform — it is not a cold start.

Current limitation: every trust signal — MSME readiness, creator contribution, editorial validation — lives off-chain in a private database. 3.2 Target Users and Validation Scope
Target users — four groups plus the chapter that seeds them:
•MSMEs — tourism MSMEs (food, craft, and experience providers) who need a verifiable readiness identity that LGUs and national agencies can trust without phone calls and paperwork.
•Creators — Kom8ks/LIT creators (writers, photographers, comic artists) who need portable, write-once proof of published contributions — the prerequisite for any future compensation path. The Kom8ks ecosystem already counts 120 registered creators (project-reported).
•LGUs — who need to issue machine-readable readiness credentials instead of paper/PDF certifications.
•TPB/DOT — who need to read on-chain trust signals for national tourism dashboards and circuit promotion.
•Ecosystem — Stellar Philippines contributors support onboarding of the first MSMEs and creators (chapter activity, outside this budget) and put real credentials through the contracts.
 
Validation scope. This sprint validates on-chain credential issuance (an issuer-allowlisted wallet → a live credential queryable by MSME wallet), write-once contribution recording, wallet onboarding, and Soroban RPC contract-state lookup (Horizon/Stellar Expert provide transaction-level visibility). It does not validate LGU governance workflows (multi-signature issuance, revocation), compensation payout mechanics, production registry logic, or mainnet readiness — those are SCF Build Award scope.

3.5 Why Stellar & Ecosystem Impact
Stellar makes per-entity credentialing economically viable and publicly verifiable at national scale. The credential layer is built natively on Stellar developer tooling:
•Stellar technologies used: Soroban smart contracts (MSME Credential Contract, Creator Contribution Contract), StellarWalletsKit (Freighter, xBull, Albedo, LOBSTR, Hana) for client-side signing, and Soroban RPC for contract-state reads (Horizon and Stellar Expert provide transaction- and account-level visibility).
•Near-zero fees (<$0.01): credential issuance costs fractions of a cent, so credentialing thousands of MSMEs stays viable for LGU budgets.
•Sub-5-second finality: a credential is verifiable almost immediately after an LGU-authorized issuer signs it.
•On-chain growth: every credential mints measurable network activity — a funded net-new wallet, a Soroban contract invocation, and a contract-state record readable via Soroban RPC that persists and remains auditable beyond the sprint. Credentialing MSMEs and creators brings non-crypto-native users onto Stellar and directly grows Soroban invocation volume and new-wallet creation — the core on-chain KPIs this sprint reports against.
•Equitable access: tourism MSMEs and independent creators are precisely the underserved economic actors the Stellar network exists to reach; a verifiable on-chain identity gives them portable proof of economic activity — a first step toward equitable access to digital financial services.
•Ecosystem public good: the public repo ships two reusable Soroban patterns — an issuer-allowlisted credential contract and a write-once, replay-guarded contribution registry — a composable reference implementation other Stellar builders in identity, attestation, and creator-economy verticals can study, fork, and extend.
Why now. ByahéBITES Nearby — Hanapin ang kainan sa budget mo — is now being deployed with pilot LGUs, with an editorial pipeline (LIT Magazine, Kom8ks) actively producing content about real MSMEs (see https://LIT.komiksguild.com and https://kom8ks.com), with 120 registered creators already active in the Kom8ks ecosystem and 10,171 verified LIT Magazine readers consuming its output (traction figures are project-reported). The missing piece is exactly the verifiable identity layer this sprint builds. Setting the credential convention now — before national circuit rollout — is far cheaper than retrofitting on-chain identity after off-chain registries have hardened.
Policy window. National policy now compels the demand side this sprint serves. The DOT Food and Gastronomy Tourism Strategic Framework and Roadmap 2024–2029 (launched May 2025) mandates culinary-zone mapping and food-circuit curation with LGUs; RA 12254, the E-Governance Act (signed September 5, 2025; IRR in force since April 2026), directs DICT to provide ready-to-use eLGU systems and requires LGUs to adopt them; and a DILG Memorandum Circular (January 2026) directs LGUs to digitalize and streamline tourism-enterprise services. The ByahéBITES LGU console is built to answer these mandates — and every verification LGUs are now directed to perform digitally is a candidate to become an on-chain credential rather than another private database entry.

3.8 Architecture & Trust Model
The full ByahéBITES architecture runs in three layers (see diagram below): a Content Layer (LIT Magazine editorial engine, Kom8ks creator amplification), a Platform Layer (ByahéBITES.app registry/discovery, LGU Console verification, TPB/DOT national console), and a Stellar Layer (Soroban contracts, StellarWalletsKit, Soroban RPC). This Instaward builds only the Stellar Layer — the two Soroban contracts — plus a minimal standalone demo. The LIT, Kom8ks, LGU Console, TPB/DOT Console, and ByahéBITES.app components in the diagram are existing or roadmap systems and are explicitly out of scope.

Figure 1 — ByahéBITES three-layer architecture. This Instaward builds only the Stellar Layer (bottom).
Known limitation, stated plainly: for this sprint, the LGU verification role is represented by a single demo issuer key held by the team, and credential issuance is permissioned to an issuer allowlist in the contract. Real LGU-console workflows — multi-signature LGU issuance, credential revocation governance, and issuer rotation — are named roadmap work for the SCF Build Award and are out of scope for this 30-day sprint. The contracts bound what the issuer key can do (issue credentials with declared attributes; nothing else — it cannot move funds, and contribution records are write-once), and every action lands on the public ledger where anyone can audit it. Issuer multisig, issuer rotation, revocation authority, and dispute processes are future work — this prototype is not decentralized institutional governance, and “LGU-verified” here means issued under the demo key standing in for the future LGU role, not an official LGU attestation.
Privacy boundary and public schema. The only data written on-chain is the minimal public credential schema: issuer address, MSME or creator wallet address, PSGC jurisdiction code, readiness-status enum, content commitment (SHA-256 hash of the off-chain canonical record), issued-ledger sequence number, a reserved revoked-ledger placeholder field, and schema version. Personal data, unpublished editorial content, private LGU assessments, raw creator submissions, business details, and platform records stay off-chain in the private platform. A content commitment proves only that a submitted canonical record matches the recorded hash — it does not prove authorship, copyright ownership, factual accuracy, LGU approval, or publication status. Credentials are non-expiring in this sprint.
Revocation model (this sprint). The schema reserves the revoked-ledger field as a placeholder only — the sprint contracts expose no revocation function. Revocation authority, multisig governance, issuer rotation, and dispute processes are SCF Build scope, stated plainly so the sprint is not read as a complete revocation system. This 30‑day prototype does not implement revocation. A placeholder field is included for future governance design. 
Credential identity & lookup. Each MSME wallet holds one current credential record, keyed by wallet address; re-issuance by an allowlisted issuer updates the record in place, and prior states remain auditable in testnet transaction history. Contribution records are keyed (creator_wallet, content_hash): the same creator cannot record the same content twice, a second attempt with an identical key is rejected on-chain, and one creator may record many distinct contents. Verifiers read records directly by key — no ambiguous selection.
Canonical serialization & hashing. Content commitments are SHA-256 digests over a domain-separated canonical serialization (UTF-8 encoding, fixed field ordering, NFC Unicode normalization, domain-separation prefix “byahebites-credential-v1”). The full specification and cross-language test vectors (Rust + TypeScript) ship in the public repo, so on-chain hashes and off-chain recomputation are guaranteed to agree.
Issuer-key model (testnet). The issuer is a standard Stellar account address held in the contract's issuer allowlist; authorization is enforced on-chain by require_auth on the issuer address — no credential can be issued without the issuer's signature. The allowlist is administered by the contract admin (the team's deployer key), which can add or remove issuer addresses; this centralized-for-testnet arrangement is disclosed openly and is replaced by multisig governance in the SCF Build phase. The testnet secret key is supplied to deployment and lifecycle scripts via environment variable or local secret manager and never enters the repository (the repo ships a .env.example placeholder and a .gitignore covering it).
Note on the architecture diagram: the “Soroban contribution credential” arrows in the diagram refer to the write-once contribution credentials built in this sprint — non-transferable records, not tokens. “Compensation payout → Creator Wallet” is roadmap functionality and is not built or funded by this Instaward. The “Horizon API” element in the diagram denotes ledger-query tooling generally: in this sprint, contract-state reads use Soroban RPC, while Horizon and Stellar Expert provide transaction- and account-level visibility.

6.4 Risks & Mitigations
•Risk: pilot-LGU issuers are not available during the sprint window. Mitigation: the demo issuer key (disclosed in §3.8) issues credentials for real MSMEs already in the pilot pipeline; LGU multi-sig issuance is roadmap work, not a sprint dependency.
•Risk: Soroban contract development takes longer than the two-week contract phase. Mitigation: both contracts are single-purpose with no token economics; the write-once and issuer-allowlist patterns are well-documented Soroban idioms, and the demo frontend reuses StellarWalletsKit reference code.
•Risk: participant availability is outside engineering control. Mitigation: the engineering deliverables (contracts, demo, test suite, documentation, evidence package) are committed unconditionally and are not blocked by onboarding delays; participant-recorded credentials are conditional on consent, chapter-supported onboarding (chapter activity, not funded here), and qualifying-content completion. Recruitment draws on the pilot-LGU pipeline and the existing 120-member Kom8ks ecosystem (project-reported) — activation of an existing base, not cold recruitment.
•Risk: reviewers cannot verify evidence without technical help. Mitigation: every deliverable ships with a tx hash list and a 3–5 minute screen-captured walkthrough narrated for a non-technical audience; Stellar Expert links require no wallet or code knowledge.
•Risk: the live demo URL becomes unavailable during review (hosting or a single RPC endpoint fails).
